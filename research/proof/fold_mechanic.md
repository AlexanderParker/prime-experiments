## 0. Pre-registered (written before any script of this branch ran; the scorecard is filled in section 7)

### 0.1 The objects, by construction

- **The line** is the counting numbers. **The fold** is the two gears `2, 3`; its **survivors** are the
  numbers neither strikes: `S = {1, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 25, ...}`, i.e. every `6j - 1` and `6j + 1`.
- **The two classes.** A survivor `n` is in **class `+1`** if `n = 6j + 1` and in **class `-1`** if
  `n = 6j - 1`. Write `chi(n) = +1` or `-1` for the class. Since `(6a + s)(6b + t) = 6(6ab + at + bs) + st`,
  the class of a product is the product of the classes: `chi(mn) = chi(m) chi(n)`. So class `+1`
  survivors form a monoid `S_+ = {1, 7, 13, 19, 25, 31, ...}`, and `S_- = {5, 11, 17, 23, 29, 35, ...}` is
  the class `-1` survivors; `S_- . S_- = S_+`, `S_+ . S_- = S_-`.
- **Column** `j` is the pair `(6j - 1, 6j + 1)`: its **left member** `L_j = 6j - 1` is in class `-1`, its
  **right member** `R_j = 6j + 1` in class `+1`. Every survivor above 1 is a member of exactly one column.
- **A gear** is a prime `g >= 5`; it is itself a survivor, so it has a class `eps_g = chi(g)` and sits in
  a column `k_g` (`g = 6 k_g + eps_g`). The **class `-1` gears** are `P_- = {5, 11, 17, 23, 29, 41, ...}`,
  the **class `+1` gears** are `P_+ = {7, 13, 19, 31, 37, 43, ...}`. Gear `g` **strikes** the survivor `n`
  iff `g` divides `n`; the survivors it strikes are the **dilate** `g.S` (origin_mechanic.md D1).
- **Left strike, right strike.** Gear `g` strikes column `j` **on the left** if `g | 6j - 1`, **on the
  right** if `g | 6j + 1`. No gear strikes both members of one column (it would divide 2).
- **The engine** at the cut `p'` is `{5..q}`, the gears up to `q = prevprime(p')`. **The section**
  `[p^2, p'^2)` of the construction (skeleton 4) is the columns `a + 1 .. b - 1`, `6a + 1 = p^2`,
  `6b + 1 = p'^2`; the **finer section** at a prime `p` is the same with `p' = nextprime(p)`.
  A section is **fully struck** if every column of it is struck by some gear of its engine.
- **Step 8 on the real machine** (skeleton 8): no section of the construction is fully struck.
- **A machine generated by a set of primes.** Let `G` be a set of primes `>= 5` and `M_G` the monoid
  it generates (every product of elements of `G`, `1` included). Its **line** is `M_G`; its **gears**
  are the irreducibles of `M_G`, which are exactly `G` (unique factorisation); gear `g` strikes
  `n in M_G` iff `g | n`; its **columns** are the pairs `(n, n + 2)` with both members in `M_G`; its
  **cuts** are `c_1 = min G`, `p_k` = the least element of `G` at or above `c_k`, `c_{k+1} = p_k^2`;
  its **sections** are `[c_k, c_{k+1})`, and its **finer sections** are `[g^2, g'^2)` for consecutive
  `g < g'` in `G`. **The square-root rule** holds on it: `n in M_G` below `p_{k+1}^2` with no factor in
  `G` below `p_{k+1}` is in `G` (else `n = ab`, `a <= b`, `a in M_G`, `a` has a factor in `G` at most
  `sqrt(n) < p_{k+1}`). **The hand-up** holds on it: the gears in section `k + 1` are exactly the
  elements of `M_G` in the section that no gear below `p_{k+1}` strikes. **Step 8 on `M_G`**: every
  section (and, for the finer form, every finer section) holds a column both of whose members are
  gears, a **twin gear pair** `(t, t + 2)`, `t, t + 2 in G`.
- **The monoid `M`** of origin_mechanic.md 6.2 is `M_G` for `G = {5} u P_+`.
- **A thinning.** `G = P_+ u T` with `T` a subset of `P_-`. The thinnings tested are listed in 0.3.
- **The twin lowers.** `W = {t : t and t + 2 both prime, t >= 5}`; every such `t` is in `P_-`
  (`t, t + 2` coprime to 6 forces `t = 5 (mod 6)`), and `t + 2 in P_+`.
- **The class-count sign.** For a survivor `n`, `Omega_-(n)` is the number of class `-1` prime factors
  of `n` counted with multiplicity, `Omega_+(n)` the number of class `+1` prime factors;
  `Omega = Omega_+ + Omega_-`. Since `chi` is multiplicative and `chi(g) = -1` exactly for the class
  `-1` primes, `chi(n) = (-1)^{Omega_-(n)}`: **the class of a survivor is the parity of its count of
  class `-1` prime factors.** The Liouville sign is `lambda(n) = (-1)^{Omega(n)}`, and the column
  sign of length_face.md is `sigma(j) = lambda(L_j) lambda(R_j)`.

### 0.2 Theory (stated before measuring)

- **T1 (the class reading of `M`).** Every irreducible of `M = M_{{5} u P_+}` other than 5 is in class
  `+1`; two class `+1` numbers differ by a multiple of 6, never by 2; so `M` has no twin gear pair
  beyond `(5, 7)`, and its failure of 8 is a class failure: a twin gear pair needs a gear from each
  class of the fold. In `M` the left member of every column is `5^{odd} . u` with `u in M_+`, struck by
  gear 5. The sharpened axiom the brief proposes: **"the gears come from both classes of the fold, at
  every scale."**
- **T2 (the reduction, to be proved in section 2).** For `G = P_+ u T`, the twin gear pairs of `M_G`
  are exactly the twin prime pairs `(t, t + 2)` with `t in T`; so 8 holds on a section of `M_G` iff
  `T` contains a twin lower inside that section. The lane expects this to make every thinned
  counter-machine transparent: 8 on it is decided by which twin lowers `T` keeps, and by nothing
  else. Consequence, if T2 holds: the sharpened axiom of T1 is **necessary and not sufficient**;
  the thinning `T = P_- \ W` (every class `-1` prime that is not a twin lower) has gears of both
  classes at every scale and no twin gear pair anywhere.
- **T3 (the classes are invisible to the column cover).** Gear `g` strikes the left member of column `j`
  iff `6j = 1 (mod g)`, i.e. `j = 6^{-1} (mod g)`, and the right member iff `j = -6^{-1} (mod g)`,
  for every gear regardless of class. The class of `g` decides only which of the two residues is
  the small one: `6^{-1} = k_g` for class `-1` (the home strike is a left strike), `6^{-1} = g - k_g`
  for class `+1` (the home strike is a right strike; the left tooth is the column of `5g`). So the
  column cover `{j : j = +-6^{-1} (mod g) for some g}` does not depend on the classes; the sides do.
- **T4 (the division of work, below `p'^2`).** The composite left members are exactly
  `P_- . (S_+ \ {1})`; the composite right members are exactly `P_- . S_- u P_+ . (S_+ \ {1})`.
  The class `-1` gears of the engine alone leave open, inside the section, exactly the left members
  `t . u` with `t in P_-`, `t > q`, `u in M_+` (the monoid generated by `P_+`) and exactly the right
  members in `M_+`; the class `+1` gears alone leave open exactly the left members in
  `M_-^{odd} . ({1} u P_+^{>q})` and the right members in `M_-^{even} . ({1} u P_+^{>q})`, where
  `M_-^{odd / even}` are the products of an odd / even number of class `-1` primes.
- **T5 (the fold's sign is visible).** `chi(n) = (-1)^{Omega_-(n)}` is the residue of `n` mod 3
  (`+1` iff `n = 1 (mod 3)`), a periodic function: sieve-visible, unlike `lambda`. The invisible sign is
  `(-1)^{Omega_+(n)} = lambda(n) chi(n)`. The column sign is `sigma(j) = -(-1)^{Omega_+(L_j) + Omega_+(R_j)}`,
  so the parity twin `O^-` of length_face.md is the set of open columns whose class `+1` factor count
  is even; the fold contributes the fixed factor `-1` and no parity content.
- **T6 (the expected verdict).** The proof attempt from the two classes ends at "a class `-1` prime
  and a class `+1` prime at distance 2", the root. The obstruction is the parity problem again; the
  two-class structure adds no obstruction and no lever of its own; what the thinnings add is that
  the missing axiom cannot be any property of the gear set that `P_+ u (P_- \ W)` shares with the
  primes (both classes at every scale, every admissible residue class at every modulus with the
  primes' density, dilation, hand-up, square-root rule, the side-swap rule): it must be a property
  of the line (every `j` is a column with both members in `S`), i.e. the strike-class law's first
  half, i.e. the cover statement at phase zero, which LF3 / LF4 block.

## 1. The two classes as a construction

Everything in this section is proved by the construction written next to it; the measured checks
(`fm_gate.py`, `fm_classes.py`) are gates on the scripts, not evidence for the root.

**F1 (the class reading of `M`).** `M` is generated by `5` and `P_+`. Its irreducibles are `5` and `P_+`.
An element of `M` is `5^a . u` with `u in M_+` (the monoid generated by `P_+`, all of it in class `+1`),
so its class is `(-1)^a`: a left member of a column of `M` has `a` odd, hence is divisible by 5, hence
is irreducible only if it is 5 itself. So the only twin gear pair of `M` is `(5, 7)`, at the base, and
no section of `M` holds one. Measured (P1, P2): in `(25, 961)` all 74 irreducibles are `= 1 (mod 6)` and
all 20 columns have `5 | L`; in `(961, 935089)` all 36,812 and all 9,985. **The reading is right**: `M`
fails 8 because a twin gear pair needs a gear from each class of the fold and `M` has, beyond 5,
gears of one class only. PROVED and measured.

**F2 (the two teeth of a gear, by class).** Gear `g` strikes the left member `6j - 1` iff `6j = 1
(mod g)` iff `j = 6^{-1} (mod g)`, and the right member iff `j = -6^{-1} (mod g)`. This holds for every
gear; the class enters only through which representative is small. Since `6 k_g = g - eps_g`,
`6 k_g = -eps_g (mod g)`, so `6^{-1} = -eps_g k_g (mod g)`: **the left tooth is `k_g` for a class `-1`
gear and `g - k_g` for a class `+1` gear, and the right tooth is the mirror.** In numbers, the left
tooth is the column of the smallest class `-1` multiple of `g` (`g` itself if `g` is class `-1`, else
`5g`) and the right tooth the column of the smallest class `+1` multiple (`g` if class `+1`, else `5g`):

PROVED.

**F3 (the classes are invisible to the column cover).** Column `j` is struck by `g` iff `j = +-6^{-1}
(mod g)`, the same two residues whichever class `g` has; the union over the engine is the real-teeth
cover of length_face.md LF1 and does not mention the classes. A machine with every gear's sides
swapped (left tooth `-6^{-1}`, right tooth `6^{-1}`) strikes exactly the same columns. So at the level
of columns, and therefore for step 8 (a column left unstruck), **the two classes of gears contribute
nothing beyond the real teeth**; what they decide is which member of a struck column is struck.
PROVED (the definitions).

**F4 (the composites of each side, below `p'^2`).** A left member `L` (class `-1`) has an odd number of
class `-1` prime factors, so at least one, `t`; then `L = t . u` with `u` in class `+1`, and `L` is
composite iff `u > 1`. So the composite left members are exactly `P_- . (S_+ \ {1})`. A right member
`R` has an even number of class `-1` prime factors: if at least two, `R = t . m` with `t in P_-`,
`m in S_-`; if none, `R in M_+` and is composite iff it is not a class `+1` prime. So the composite
right members are exactly `P_- . S_- u P_+ . (S_+ \ {1})`. **Every composite left member carries a
class `-1` prime; a composite right member need not** (`R = 7 . 7 = 49`, `7 . 13 = 91`, ...). PROVED.

**F5 (the division of work between the two classes, below `p'^2`).** Peel the class `-1` gears of the
engine `{5..q}` first. A left member they leave open has every class `-1` prime factor above `q`; it
has an odd number of them, and since `L < p'^2` and there is no prime in `(q, p')`, two class `-1`
factors above `q` would multiply to at least `p'^2 > L`; hence exactly one, `t > q`, and `L = t . u` with `u in M_+` (every class `+1` factor of `u` is below `p'^2 / t < p'`, so
`<= q`). A right member they leave open has no class `-1` factor `<= q`, hence (same size argument, the
count being even) none at all: `R in M_+`. So **after the class `-1` gears the open left members of the
section are `P_-^{>q} . M_+` and the open right members are `M_+`.** The class `+1` gears then strike
`t . u` iff `u > 1` and `v in M_+` iff `v` is composite. Symmetrically, after the class `+1` gears alone
the open left members are `M_-^{odd} . ({1} u P_+^{>q})` and the open right members
`M_-^{even} . ({1} u P_+^{>q})` (at most one class `+1` factor above `q` fits below `p'^2`). PROVED;
measured on 18 real sections with 0 exceptions (P6, table below).

**F6 (the exact cover condition in the two classes).** The section `[p^2, p'^2)` is fully struck iff
for every column `j` of it
`L_j in P_-^{<=q} . S_+ u P_+^{<=q} . S_-` or `R_j in P_-^{<=q} . S_- u P_+^{<=q} . S_+`;
below `p'^2` this reads: `L_j` is composite or `R_j` is composite; and by F5 it reads: **for every
class `-1` prime `t` in the section, `t + 2` is a composite element of `M_+` or has two class `-1`
factors.** The left teeth of the class `-1` gears cover the left members `t . u` with `t <= q`; the
left teeth of the class `+1` gears cover the left members with a class `+1` factor `<= q`; what neither
covers on the left are the class `-1` primes above `q`, which exist in every section (prior art,
one line: primes in both classes mod 6 in every `[x, 2x]`, Breusch 1932 / Erdos 1935; the section is
far longer than `[x, 2x]`; stopped). So the statement the brief asked to look for, "the left teeth of
the class `-1` gears cannot cover the left members of a section because ...", is true with the reason
"because a class `-1` prime lies in the section", and it is not what 8 needs: 8 needs the right
partner of one of those primes to be open too, and F6 says the two classes decide nothing about that
pairing beyond the definitions. PROVED (the reduction) and STOPPED (the prime-existence half).

**F7 (the fold's sign is visible; the parity content is in class `+1`).** `chi(n) = (-1)^{Omega_-(n)}`
and `chi(n) = +1` iff `n = 1 (mod 3)`: the sign that counts class `-1` prime factors is the residue
mod 3, a periodic function, the first thing any sieve on `S` knows. The Liouville sign factors as
`lambda(n) = chi(n) . (-1)^{Omega_+(n)}`, so the sieve-invisible part of `lambda` on `S` is the parity
of the class `+1` factor count. For a column, `Omega_-(L_j)` is odd and `Omega_-(R_j)` even, so
`sigma(j) = lambda(L_j) lambda(R_j) = -(-1)^{Omega_+(L_j) + Omega_+(R_j)}`: **the parity twin `O^-` of
length_face.md is the set of open columns whose total class `+1` factor count is even**, and the fold
contributes the constant `-1` and no parity content. So "a Liouville-type sign restricted to one
class" is the wrong reading in one direction: the sign restricted to class `-1` is fully visible
(it is the class itself), and the invisible sign is the one restricted to class `+1`. PROVED.

## 2. The counter-machines and the weakest thinning

### 2.1 The reduction (T2), proved

**Lemma.** Let `G = P_+ u T`, `T` a subset of `P_-`. A twin gear pair of `M_G` is a column both of
whose members are in `G`. Both members are then primes at distance 2, i.e. a twin prime pair
`(t, t + 2)` with `t >= 5`; `t = 5 (mod 6)` and `t + 2 = 1 (mod 6)`, so `t + 2 in P_+ subset G` always
and `t in G` iff `t in T`. Conversely every twin prime pair with `t in T` has both members in `G`.

member in `T`; and by the square-root rule and the hand-up (0.1) a column of `M_G` in a section that
no gear below the section's cut generator strikes is a twin gear pair. Hence **8 holds on a section
of `M_G` iff `T` contains the lower member of a twin prime pair inside that section.** QED.

## 3. Mechanism

**What the two classes are, exactly.** The class is the residue mod 3 on `S`, multiplicative, and
the gears' classes are their own residues. A gear's two teeth are `+-6^{-1} (mod g)`, one on each
side; which side is which is the gear's class, and the cover of columns does not see it (F3). So the
owner's picture is exact and proved in this form: the fold's left / right pattern (every column has a
left and a right member, both in `S`) is preserved by every dilate (a gear never strikes both members
of a column; `g . S_+` lands on one side, `g . S_-` on the other, F2); each gear's own left / right
pattern is one left strike and one right strike per `g` columns at mirror residues; and the union of
those patterns covers "some of the fold's runs" exactly as the real-teeth cover does. The classes
add to that cover nothing: the runs it covers are the same runs.

**What the classes do decide** is which member is struck, and there F4 / F5 are the whole content:
a composite left member always carries a class `-1` prime; a composite right member may not; after
the class `-1` gears the open left members are `P_-^{>q} . M_+` and the open right members `M_+`. A
fully struck section is one in which every class `-1` prime `t` of the section has `t + 2` composite.
That is the definition of "no twin prime in the section" written with the classes named, and the
thinned machines show it is the only thing the classes say: a machine with exactly this two-class
structure and no twin gear pair is obtained by removing the twin lowers, and nothing else about it
changes (both classes at every scale, every residue class of every modulus with the primes' density
since the twin lowers have relative density 0 among the primes by Brun's bound, prior art, one line,
stopped; dilation, hand-up, square-root rule, side swap, F4 / F5 verbatim).

**Why the monoid `M` fails, in the mechanism's words.** Its only class `-1` generator is 5, and
every other generator `t = 1 (mod 6)` has `t + 2 = 3 (mod 6)`, struck by the fold's own gear 3: the
pairing is killed by a congruence at the fold's own modulus. One modulus up, `T = {23 (mod 30)}` has the pairing killed by 5, and
`{5 (mod 42)}` by 7. The whole chain of such congruence machines is excluded by the primes'
equidistribution in every admissible class mod every modulus (Dirichlet, prior art, one line,
stopped), and the fiat thinning is not excluded by it. So no axiom on the classes of the gears, at
any modulus, is the missing one.

**Where the fold is used, then.** In the construction the fold appears in exactly one statement
(skeleton 2): every column `j` has both members in `S`, and a gear strikes columns `+-k_g`. The
thinned machines all keep the second half and break the first (their columns are the pairs with
both members in `M_G`, a thin set). The real machine's line is all of `S`; step 8 is "the gears
`{5..q}` do not cover the columns `a + 1 .. b - 1`"; and that is the covering statement at phase
zero, length_face.md 4.3, whose parity twin `O^-` violates it with sieve-indistinguishable data
(LF3 / LF4). The sign the fold defines (F7) is visible and enters `O^-` only as the constant `-1`.

## 4. Proof attempt, and the exact obstruction

**4.1 The attempt.** Take the section `[p^2, p'^2)`, engine `{5..q}`, `q = prevprime(p')`, and suppose
it fully struck. Peel the class `-1` gears (F5): the columns left open are those with `L = t . u`,
`t in P_-^{>q}`, `u in M_+`, and `R in M_+`. Among them are the columns whose left member is a class `-1`
prime of the section (`u = 1`); such primes exist (Breusch, stopped). Now the class `+1` gears must
strike every such column, i.e. every such `R = t + 2` must be a composite element of `M_+` or, if it
has class `-1` factors, a product `t' . m` with `t' <= q` (F4). So: **for every class `-1` prime `t` in
`(p^2, p'^2)`, `t + 2` is composite.** The two classes have been used completely (F2-F7) and the
statement reached is the negation of 8 in its own words. To go on one must use that the columns
`j` range over ALL of `a + 1 .. b - 1`, i.e. that `t + 2` for a prime `t` is a member of the line with
the ordinary density of primes, which is the cover statement at phase zero (LF1 at `t = 0`), the
length face, ROOT (length_face.md 4.3). Stop line: primes of both classes in the section is
Breusch; the pairing is the root; no third input exists in the two-class structure (F3).

**4.2 The exact obstruction, as a construction with its smallest instance.** Any argument that uses
only: dilation (strikes are multiples), the hand-up, the square-root rule, the two classes of the
gears with both present at every scale and in every residue class of every modulus with the primes'
density, the side-swap rule, and F4-F7, applies verbatim to `M_G` with `G = P_+ u (P_- \ W)` (replace
"prime" by "generator", "survivor" by "element of `M_G`"), where its conclusion is false at every
section, the smallest being `[49, 2809)`: 85 columns, 0 twin gear pairs, the first twelve listed in
2.3 (4). What `M_G` lacks is one thing: its line is not all of `S`; in particular the column
`(t, t + 2)` for a twin lower `t` is not a column of `M_G` at all. So the proof must use that every
`j` is a column of the real machine, i.e. the strike-class law's first half, and that is the cover
statement at phase zero.

**Is it the parity problem again?** Two obstructions have been exhibited and they are different
objects. (i) The fiat thinning is NOT a parity example: its line is not sieve-indistinguishable from
`S`, it is a thin subset, and what it shows is that **no property of the gear set, sieve-visible or
not, short of "contains a twin pair in the section", can be the missing axiom**; the axiom must be
a property of the line. (ii) Once the line is all of `S`, the statement is the cover at phase zero,
and its obstruction is the parity twin `O^-` of length_face.md, which IS Selberg's parity example in
these coordinates (LF3 / LF4, measured at eight cuts). The two-class sign does not change that
example: `O^-` is the set of open columns with an even class `+1` factor count (F7), the class `-1`
count being fixed by the side. So: **the parity problem again, with the two-class structure shown
to be on the visible side of it**, and one new brick (i) in front of it.

**What is NOT excluded**, stated as a construction: a use of "every `j` is a column" that is not a
free-phase cover and not a count. The strongest exhibited non-count protection on record remains
the one-gear pin at `p = 29` (origin_mechanic.md section 3), which is a fact about which composites
exist, and this lane found no two-class version of it: the classes of the pins `g x m` are
determined by the classes of `g` and `m` and add no constraint on their positions.

## 6. Verdict

**ROOT, with the two-class structure closed as a lever.** The class reading of the monoid is right
(F1); the sharpened axiom "both classes at every scale" is necessary and not sufficient, refuted by
`P_+ u (P_- \ W)` at `[49, 2809)`; every monoid generated by `P_+` and a subset of `P_-` decides 8 by
which twin lowers it keeps (the Lemma, 0 mismatches at 7,309 sections), so the family carries no
mechanic; the classes are invisible to the column cover (F3) and decide only which member is struck
(F4 / F5); the fold's sign is the visible character mod 3 and the parity problem sits entirely in
the class `+1` factor count (F7); the proof attempt from the two classes ends at "every class `-1`
prime of the section has a composite `+ 2`", the root in its own words (4.1). The obstruction is the
parity problem again (4.2 (ii)), with one new brick in front of it: the missing axiom cannot be a
property of the gear set at all, it must be a property of the line (every `j` is a column), and
that is the cover statement at phase zero. Where the difficulty moved: from "a use of the fold that
is not the strike-class law" to "a use of the strike-class law's FIRST half (every column has both
members in `S`) that is neither a free-phase cover nor a count"; the second half (the teeth) and the
classes are exhausted.
