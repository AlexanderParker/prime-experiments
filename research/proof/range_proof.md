## 1. Definitions

Every term is defined here once and used afterwards without redefinition.

**1.1 Numbers.** All numbers are natural numbers. *Prime* is `Nat.Prime`. For n the *primorial* n# (Mathlib `primorial`) is the product of the primes at most n. Values used below: 5# = 30, 7# = 210, 11# = 2310, 13# = 30030, 23# = 223092870, 29# = 6469693230, 31# = 200560490130, 41# = 304250263527210 [certificate: exact multiplication; 7# = 210 is also kernel `primorial_seven`]. The primorial is monotone (Lemma 1). For a prime p, p divides n# exactly when p ≤ n [Mathlib `Nat.Prime.dvd_primorial_iff`]. *isqrt(n)* = `Nat.sqrt n`, the largest x with x² ≤ n. *minFac(n)* = `Nat.minFac n`, the least prime factor of n ≥ 2. *nextprime(n)* is the least prime above n; it exists [Mathlib `Nat.exists_infinite_primes`] and is written q' when n = q (kernel `nextGear q`, `nextGear_spec`, `nextGear_le`).

**1.2 Columns.** For n ≥ 1, *column* n is the pair (6n − 1, 6n + 1). Every twin pair (p, p + 2) with p ≥ 5 is a column [argument: p and p + 2 are primes above 3, so neither is divisible by 3; p ≡ 1 (mod 3) would give 3 | p + 2, so p ≡ 2 (mod 3); p is odd; hence p ≡ 5 (mod 6) and p = 6n − 1].

**1.3 Copies and legs.** For j ≥ 1, *copy* j is the pair (30j − 1, 30j + 1); it is column 5j. Its *lower leg* is 30j − 1 and its *upper leg* is 30j + 1. Both legs are coprime to 30. Throughout, j ≥ 1: copy 0 (legs −1 and 1) is excluded. The copies are exactly the columns whose lower leg is 29 modulo 30.

**1.4 Gears, machine, period, range.** A *gear* is a prime g ≥ 7. The primes 2, 3, 5 lay out the copies and are not gears. *Machine* q (q ≥ 7) is the set of gears 7..q; q is the *size* of the machine and its *top gear* when q is prime. The *period* of machine q is q#. Write M = M_q = q#/30 (kernel `mirrorM q`; also `gearProd q`, the product of the gears 7..q, with `thirty_mul_gearProd`: 30·gearProd q = q# for q ≥ 5). For q ≥ 5: 30M = q# [kernel `thirty_mul_mirrorM`], M is odd [kernel `odd_mirrorM`], and 7 ≤ M for q ≥ 7 [kernel `seven_le_mirrorM`]. The *range* of machine q is the set of copies j with q < 30j − 1 and 30j + 1 ≤ q#: the copies whose two legs both lie in the interval (q, q#]. In index form, for q ≥ 5 the range is {j : ⌊(q + 1)/30⌋ + 1 ≤ j ≤ M − 1} (4.3, Step 2). A *range copy* is a member of the range.

**1.5 Strike.** g *strikes* copy j when g divides (30j − 1)(30j + 1) [kernel `StrikesCopy g j`], equivalently g | 900j² − 1 [kernel `strikesCopy_iff_sq`]. For a prime g this is "g divides a leg" [kernel `strikesCopy_iff_leg`, `copy_strike_iff_legs`]. For a prime g ≥ 7 and j ≥ 1, g strikes j exactly when j ≡ a_g or j ≡ −a_g (mod g), where a_g = 30⁻¹ mod g [kernel `strikes_iff_gearRes`]; these are two distinct residues. A prime dividing a leg of a copy j ≥ 1 is at least 7 [kernel `Gen4.seven_le_of_dvd_leg`]; 2, 3, 5 never strike a copy.

**1.6 Acting.** g *acts on* copy j when g² ≤ 30j + 1 [kernel `Acts g j`]. Acting is monotone in j [kernel `acts_mono`]. For a divisor h ≥ 2 of a leg L: h acts exactly when h ≤ L/h [kernel `Gen4.acts_iff_le_cofactor`]; so an acting prime factor of a leg is the smaller factor. A *home copy* of g is a copy one of whose legs equals g [kernel `Gen4.Home g j`]; a home strike never acts [kernel `Gen4.home_not_acts`]. A prime g *deletes* copy j at machine q when g > q, g strikes j and g acts on j [kernel `Deleted q j`].

**1.7 Survivor.** Copy j *survives* machine q, or is *clear of* the gears 7..q, when no prime p with 7 ≤ p ≤ q strikes j. The kernel has four names for this one predicate: `Gen4.Surv q j`, `Survives q j` (RangeGen3), `Clear q j` (RangeGen1) and `FreeClear q j` (RangeGen5, with `InGears X g := g.Prime ∧ 7 ≤ g ∧ g ≤ X`); the ledger's reviewer checked that they agree. S_q is the set of survivors of machine q.

**1.8 Revealed copy, twin copy, least prime factor.** Copy j is *revealed*, or a *twin copy*, when both legs are prime [kernel `Gen4.Twin j`, `TwinCopy j`, `LegsPrime j`, `Twin j` in RangeGen1: the same predicate]. P(j) = minFac(900j² − 1), the least prime factor of the product of the legs [kernel `P`, `P_eq_minFac_legs`]. For j ≥ 1: j is a twin copy exactly when 30j + 1 < P(j)² [kernel `revealed_iff_minFac`]; the negation, P(j)² ≤ 30j + 1, says that the least prime factor of a leg acts, and then j is *blamed on* P(j) [kernel `blamed_iff_minFac`]. Acting form: for 1 ≤ j with 30j + 1 ≤ q#, j is a twin copy exactly when no acting gear of [7, isqrt(q#)] strikes it [kernel `actClear_iff_legsPrime_range`]. A *revealed range copy* of machine q is a range copy that is a twin copy; the kernel's `Revealed q j` (RangeGen1: 1 ≤ j ≤ M − 1, Clear q j, Twin j) is the same thing for q ≥ 7 [kernel `revealed_iff_twin_above`].

**1.9 Cuts and upper gears.** X = X_q = isqrt(q#) [kernel `cutSqrt q`]. U_q is the set of primes g with q < g ≤ X [kernel `Gen4.InU q g`]: the *upper gears* that can act on a range copy. ρ = q#/2 + 1 = 15M + 1 [kernel `mirrorRho`, `mirror_data`]. U⁻ is the set of primes g > q with g² ≤ ρ [kernel `uMinusSet`].

**1.10 Missed copy.** For a prime g ≥ 7, g² mod 30 is 1 or 19, and exactly one of the two [kernel `sq_mod30_cases`, `sq_mod30_exactly_one`]. *Case 1* (g² ≡ 1): the offsets are (A_g, B_g) = (28, 30) and J_g = (g² + 29)/30. *Case 19* (g² ≡ 19): (A_g, B_g) = (10, 12) and J_g = (g² + 11)/30 [kernel `missedA`, `missedB`, `MissedOffsets`, `missedOffsets_missed`]. In both cases B_g = A_g + 2. The *missed copy* c_g is copy J_g; its legs are g² + A_g and g² + B_g [kernel `missed_copy_legs`, `missed_copy_legs_case1`, `missed_copy_legs_case19`]. A prime h *kills* c_g when h divides a leg of c_g. g is a *revealed gear* when c_g is a twin copy.

**1.11 Mirror pairs.** For q ≥ 5 and odd d < M, the *mirror pair at d* has *low member* lo = (M − d)/2 [kernel `mirrorLo q d`] and *high member* hi = (M + d)/2 [kernel `mirrorHi q d`]; lo + hi = M, hi = lo + d, 1 ≤ lo [kernel `mirror_sum`, `mirrorHi_eq`, `one_le_mirrorLo`]. The *mirror* of copy x is M − x. A *survivor pair* is a mirror pair both of whose members survive machine q; it is *doubly deleted* when both members are deleted.

**1.12 Twin pairs, nodes, rungs, good nodes.** A *twin pair* is (p, p + 2) with p and p + 2 prime; its *lower leg* is p. A *twin node* is the lower leg of a twin pair [kernel `TwinNode s := s.Prime ∧ (s + 2).Prime`]. For a node s, its *successor node* s⁺ is the least node above s [kernel `NextNode s t := TwinNode t ∧ s < t ∧ ∀ u, s < u → u < t → ¬ TwinNode u`]; it is unique [kernel `nextNode_unique`]. A *rung* of s is a node r with s < r and r + 2 ≤ s# [kernel `Rung s r`]. s is *good* when s⁺ exists and s⁺ + 2 ≤ s# [kernel `Good s := ∃ t, NextNode s t ∧ t + 2 ≤ primorial s`]. *Twin-node classes*: every twin node s ≥ 7 has s mod 30 ∈ {11, 17, 29} [argument: s is odd; s ≢ 0 and s ≢ 1 (mod 3), because 3 does not divide the primes s, s + 2 > 3, so s ≡ 2 (mod 3); s mod 5 ∉ {0, 3}, because 5 does not divide the primes s, s + 2 > 5; the odd residues mod 30 that are 2 mod 3 are 5, 11, 17, 23, 29, and removing 5 (≡ 0 mod 5) and 23 (≡ 3 mod 5) leaves 11, 17, 29]. The twin nodes in class 29 are exactly the lower legs of the twin copies; classes 11 and 17 are twin pairs that are not copies, for example 41, 71, 101, 107, 137 [certificate].

**1.13 The range statement and its forms.** The *range statement at* q is RangeStatement q := ∃ p, q < p ∧ p + 2 ≤ q# ∧ p prime ∧ p + 2 prime [kernel `RangeStatement`]: some twin pair, on or off the copies, has both members in (q, q#]. RangeAll := ∀ q ≥ 7, RangeStatement q [kernel `RangeAll`]. The *copy form* at q is RangeCopyWindow q := ∃ j, 1 ≤ j ∧ q < 30j − 1 ∧ 30j + 1 ≤ q# ∧ both legs of j prime [kernel `RangeCopyWindow`]: some range copy is revealed. The kernel's `RangeCopy q` (RangeGen1: some `Revealed q j`) is the same statement for q ≥ 7 (1.8). The ledger writes Range(q) for the copy form and RANGE for "Range(q) at every prime q ≥ 7"; this document writes S1 for RANGE (3.5). A twin pair (p, p + 2) *serves* q when q < p and p + 2 ≤ q#. A *chain* is a function p : ℕ → ℕ that is strictly increasing, with every p k a twin node, p 0 = 29 and p (k + 1) + 2 ≤ (p k)# for every k. *GoodReach* is the set of nodes reachable from 29 by steps s → s⁺ taken only when s⁺ + 2 ≤ s# [kernel inductive `GoodReach`]. NS is the statement: every good node s ≥ 29 has s⁺⁺ + 2 ≤ s# [kernel `NS`].

## 2. The theorem chain

Every lemma in this section is PROVED.

**Lemma 1 (primorial is monotone).** a ≤ b gives a# ≤ b#. [Mathlib `primorial_mono`; kernel `primorial_le_of_le`.]

**Lemma 2 (two facts about primes).** (a) For n ≠ 0 there is a prime p with n < p ≤ 2n (Bertrand's postulate). [Mathlib `Nat.exists_prime_lt_and_le_two_mul`; published: P. L. Chebyshev, "Mémoire sur les nombres premiers", J. Math. Pures Appl. (1) 17 (1852), 366–390.] (b) For every n there is a prime p ≥ n. [Mathlib `Nat.exists_infinite_primes`.] (c) A composite n > 0 has minFac(n)² ≤ n. [Mathlib `Nat.minFac_sq_le_self`.]

**Lemma 3 (the base value).** 7# = 210, and x ≤ 210 gives x ≤ q# for every q ≥ 7. [kernel `primorial_seven`, `le_primorial_of_le_210`; the second from Lemma 1.]

**Lemma 4 (a twin pair serves a bound).** If p and p + 2 are prime, q < p and p + 2 ≤ q#, then RangeStatement q. [kernel `twin_serves`: the definition read backwards.]

**Lemma 5 (serving intervals).** If p and p + 2 are prime, p + 2 ≤ q₁# and q₂ < p, then RangeStatement q for every q with q₁ ≤ q ≤ q₂. [kernel `serves_interval_of_le`.] Proof: for such q, q ≤ q₂ < p, and p + 2 ≤ q₁# ≤ q# by Lemma 1; apply Lemma 4. (No primality of q₁, q₂ is needed.)

**Lemma 6 (base cases).** (a) RangeStatement q for every q with 7 ≤ q ≤ 23, witness p = 29: 29 and 31 are prime, 23 < 29, 31 ≤ 210 = 7#. [kernel `small_cases`, by Lemma 5.] (b) 29 is a twin node [kernel `twinNode_29`]. (c) 29 is good: 41 is a rung of 29, since 41 and 43 are prime, 29 < 41 and 43 ≤ 210 ≤ 29#. [kernel `good_29`.]

**Lemma 7 (the range statement at every prime gives unbounded twins).** If RangeStatement q holds for every prime q ≥ 7, then for every N there is a prime p > N with p + 2 prime. [kernel `range_implies_unbounded`.] Proof: given N, Lemma 2(b) gives a prime q ≥ N + 7; RangeStatement q gives a twin pair (p, p + 2) with p > q > N.

**Lemma 8 (a chain gives the range statement).** Let p be a chain (1.13). Then RangeStatement q for every q ≥ 7, and twin pairs are unbounded. [kernel `chain_implies_range_all`, `chain_implies_range`, `chain_implies_unbounded`.] Proof: given q ≥ 7, the set {k : q < p k} is non-empty, because strict monotonicity gives p k ≥ k, so k = q + 1 belongs to it; let k₀ be its least element. If k₀ = 0, then p 0 = 29 > q and 31 ≤ 210 ≤ q# (Lemma 3), so Lemma 4 applies. If k₀ = j + 1, then p j ≤ q by minimality, so p (j + 1) + 2 ≤ (p j)# ≤ q# by Lemma 1, and p (j + 1) > q; Lemma 4 applies. Unboundedness follows from Lemma 7.

**Lemma 9 (good, rung and range statement at a node).** For every s: Good s ⟺ some rung of s exists ⟺ RangeStatement s. [kernel `good_iff_exists_rung`, `good_iff_rangeStatement`.] Proof: if s is good, s⁺ is a rung. If r is a rung of s, then s⁺ exists [kernel `exists_nextNode`] and s⁺ ≤ r [kernel `rung_ge_next`], so s⁺ + 2 ≤ r + 2 ≤ s# [kernel `rungs_initial`]; hence s is good. A rung of s is exactly a witness of RangeStatement s, by the two definitions.

**Lemma 10 (every node good ⟺ RangeAll ⟺ the range statement at every prime).** The following are equivalent: (i) every twin node s ≥ 29 is good; (ii) RangeAll; (iii) RangeStatement q for every prime q ≥ 7. [kernel `rangeAll_of_all_good`, `all_good_of_rangePrime`, `range_iff_all_good`, `rangePrime_iff_all_good`.] Proof. (ii) ⟹ (iii): specialisation. (iii) ⟹ (i): a twin node s ≥ 29 is a prime ≥ 7, so RangeStatement s, and Lemma 9 gives Good s. (i) ⟹ (ii): let q ≥ 7. If q < 29, then p = 29 serves q by Lemma 4 and Lemma 3 (31 ≤ 210 ≤ q#). If q ≥ 29, let a be the largest twin node ≤ q [Mathlib `Nat.findGreatest`: `Nat.findGreatest_spec`, `Nat.le_findGreatest`, `Nat.findGreatest_le`; 29 ≤ a ≤ q]. By (i), a is good, so by Lemma 9 it has a rung r: a twin node with a < r and r + 2 ≤ a#. If r ≤ q, r would be a twin node in (a, q], against the maximality of a [Mathlib `Nat.findGreatest_is_greatest`]; so r > q. And a# ≤ q# by Lemma 1. Lemma 4 gives RangeStatement q.

**Lemma 11 (RangeAll gives unbounded twins).** RangeAll ⟹ ∀ N, ∃ p > N with p and p + 2 prime. [kernel `twins_unbounded_of_rangeAll`: Lemma 7 applied to the specialisation of RangeAll to primes.]

**Lemma 12 (the reach form).** (a) s ∈ GoodReach ⟺ s is a twin node ≥ 29 and every twin node u with 29 ≤ u < s is good [kernel `goodReach_iff`]. (b) The reachable nodes form an initial run of the nodes from 29 [kernel `GoodReach.initial`]. (c) GoodReach is unbounded ⟺ every twin node s ≥ 29 is good ⟺ RangeAll [kernel `reach_unbounded_iff`, `range_iff_reach_unbounded`]. (d) Every reachable number is a twin node ≥ 29 [kernel `GoodReach.node`].

**Lemma 13 (NS).** (a) NS ⟹ every twin node s ≥ 29 is good [kernel `all_good_of_ns`; the base is Lemma 6(c) and the step is `ns_step`]. (b) NS ⟹ RangeAll ⟹ twins unbounded [kernel `range_of_ns`, `twins_unbounded_of_ns`]. (c) NS ⟺ every twin node s ≥ 29 has two distinct rungs ⟺ no twin node s ≥ 29 has exactly one rung [kernel `ns_iff_two_rungs`, `ns_iff_no_single_rung`, via `second_of_two_rungs`].

**Lemma 14 (anatomy of a failure).** (a) Every twin node s > 29 is the successor node of a twin node a with 29 ≤ a < s [kernel `exists_prevNode`]. (b) A rung of s above t ≥ s is a rung of t [kernel `rung_transport`, `overlap_agree`]. (c) The rungs of s are an initial run of the nodes above s, starting at s⁺ [kernel `rungs_initial`, `rung_ge_next`]. (d) Passing from a to a⁺ loses at most the rung a⁺: every rung of a other than a⁺ is a rung of a⁺ [kernel `drop_at_most_one`]. (e) If a is good and a⁺ is not good, then a⁺ is the only rung of a [kernel `first_bad`]. (f) Some twin node ≥ 29 is not good ⟺ some twin node a ≥ 29 has exactly one rung t and t is not good [kernel `bad_iff_terminal`]. (g) A node can be a rung of two nodes: 149 is a rung of 29 and of 59 [kernel `not_parent_unique`].

**Lemma 15 (the copy form gives the range statement).** RangeCopyWindow q ⟹ RangeStatement q, with witness p = 30j − 1 [kernel `copy_range_implies_rangeStatement`, `rangeCopyWindow_rangeStatement`]; and for q ≥ 5, RangeCopy q ⟹ RangeStatement q [kernel `rangeCopy_implies_rangeStatement`]. The converse is not proved: RangeStatement q admits twin pairs off the copies (1.12), and no theorem converts such a witness into a copy in the same range.

**Lemma 16 (the least-prime-factor form).** For every q: RangeCopyWindow q ⟺ some range copy j has 30j + 1 < P(j)² [kernel `range_copy_iff`]; ¬RangeCopyWindow q ⟺ every range copy j has P(j)² ≤ 30j + 1 [kernel `no_copy_blame`]; for every bound N: no copy in [1, N) is a twin copy ⟺ every copy there has P(j)² ≤ 30j + 1 [kernel `total_blame_iff`].

**Lemma 17 (growth and the wall).** [imports Lemma 2(a) through Mathlib] (a) For every prime p with 7 ≤ p ≤ q: 2p² < q# [kernel `two_mul_sq_lt_primorial`, `prime_two_sq_lt_primorial`]. **Lemma 18 (survivors and blame; RangeGen4).** For j ≥ 1: (a) Surv q j ⟺ q < P(j) [kernel `surv_iff_lt_P`]; for q ≥ 5, Surv q j ⟺ gcd(900j² − 1, M) = 1 [kernel `surv_iff_coprime`]. (f) If no prime lies in (q, q') and j is a non-twin survivor with 30j + 1 ≤ q'², then 30j + 1 = q'², j = (q'² − 1)/30, the lower leg is prime and P(j) = q' [kernel `stratum_nonrevealed`]; such a j exists ⟺ q'² ≡ 1 (mod 30) and q'² − 2 is prime [kernel `stratum_iff`]. (g) The next gear q' strikes a survivor j ≥ 1 of machine q only by acting or at a home copy [kernel `strike_acts_or_home`, `nextGear_strike_acts_or_home`].

(b) For k ≥ 1 and gearProd X | N: FreeClear X (k + N) ⟺ FreeClear X k [kernel `freeClear_add_iff`]. **Lemma 20 (locator closure and silent classes).** (a) For a prime g not dividing N and any r, t: some k < g has r + kN ≡ t (mod g), and the g residues r + kN (k < g) are distinct [kernel `class_meets_every_residue`, `class_residues_distinct`]. **Lemma 21 (mirror pairs; RangeMirror, RangeGen3).** For q ≥ 5 and odd d < M: (a) leg sums: (30lo − 1) + (30hi + 1) = (30lo + 1) + (30hi − 1) = q#; (30lo − 1) + (30hi − 1) = q# − 2; (30lo + 1) + (30hi + 1) = q# + 2 [kernel `legs_sum`, `legs_diff`, `height_split`]. (b) A gear with q# + 2 < 2g² acts on at most one member [kernel `acts_on_at_most_one`]. ## 3. The keystone

**3.1 Statement.** K (node-gap form): for every twin node s ≥ 29, the successor node s⁺ exists and s⁺ + 2 ≤ s#. In words: consecutive twin pairs (s, s + 2) < (t, t + 2) with s ≥ 29 always have t + 2 ≤ s#; after every twin node s ≥ 29 the next twin pair lies inside (s, s#].

**3.2 Lean form.** `∀ s, TwinNode s → 29 ≤ s → Good s`, with the kernel definitions of 1.12 (RangeReach). Machine reading (Lemma 10): for every machine size q ≥ 7 some twin pair, on or off the copies, lies in the range (q, q#] of machine q.

**3.3 Why K is the keystone.** (a) Not proved: ledger 3.1 ("RANGE itself") and the range-line entry of `the_wall.md`; Section 4. (b) Sufficient: K ⟹ RangeAll (Lemma 10) ⟹ twins unbounded (Lemma 11); Section 5.
