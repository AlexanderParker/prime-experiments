# Phase saturation: a closed-form, solver-free ceiling on kill-chain arity

Round 26 (mechanic). Gate: `research/akill_verify_r26.py` -> ALL ASSERTIONS PASSED

## 1. WHAT IT IS

Plain language. When the slot machine gains a new gear q', that gear deletes some
openings of the old machine. A *kill chain* is a run of k consecutive old openings
that ONE phase of the new gear deletes in a single stroke; the longest such run,
A_kill(M -> q'), is what sets the depth the merge law has to reach. Until now the
only way to find A_kill was to enumerate candidate gap words and hand each one to a
SAT solver - hours per word at the machines that matter. This note says that a large
fraction of those words can be refuted by counting residues, and that for the one
family that has actually produced long chains - the *alternating* chain - the answer
is a closed form in q' and the small primes, with no solver at all.

The mechanism is a pigeonhole on the phases of a SMALL gear. Every gear has only q
possible phases and each phase kills a pair of residue classes; a long chain exposes
many residues; once the exposed residues (together with their translates) cover all
of Z_q, the gear has nowhere to stand.

Precise form. Fix a step M -> q'. A candidate k-chain is given by its gap word
w = (w_1, ..., w_{k-1}), with exposed offsets

    X(w) = {0, w_1, w_1 + w_2, ..., w_1 + ... + w_{k-1}}   (|X| = k).

In the CRT encoding of the machine (K1), gear q blocks the residue pair
{a, a + s_q} mod q for a free phase a, where s_q = -2 * 6^{-1} (mod q). Define

    FREE_q(X) = Z_q \ ( (X mod q) u ((X - s_q) mod q) ).

THEOREM (phase saturation). If FREE_q(X(w)) is empty for some gear q <= M, then the
word w has NO occurrence anywhere in the period of machine M: it is zero, by
arithmetic, with no solver call.

    |FREE_q(X)| >= q - 2k, so a gear q < 2k can never be discarded a priori and a
    gear q >= 2k+1 never fires; the whole content is at the SMALL gears 5, 7, 11.

COROLLARY (the alternation ceiling). Let s = 2 * 6^{-1} mod q' and let A_k be the
pure alternation (s, q'-s, s, q'-s, ...) of k-1 letters - the shape that carries
every long chain observed in this project. Then

    ceil(M -> q') := min{ k : FREE_q(X(A_k)) = {} for some q <= M } - 1

is an upper bound on the length of any alternating chain at that step, and it is a
CLOSED FORM: X(A_k) mod q is generated by 0, s and q' mod q, so the ceiling depends
only on (q' mod q, s mod q) for the two or three smallest gears.

## 2. WHY IT MIGHT BE NOVEL

- The underlying pigeonhole is elementary and certainly not new as a technique. What
  looks new is the OBJECT it is applied to and what it decides: the arity of a
  single-phase deletion chain in a Jacobsthal-type covering problem, i.e. the depth
  parameter of the merge law, which had been treated in this project as an
  arithmetically selected accident measured by SAT.
- It is NOT a restatement of the usual "a prime q can cover at most ceil(L/q)
  positions of an interval of length L" counting bound. That bound is about how much
  a gear covers; this is about whether a gear has any admissible PHASE at all given
  a prescribed set of positions it must AVOID - an avoidance condition, and the
  saturation happens at k ~ q/2, far below the covering threshold.
- The two-teeth structure (each phase blocks {a, a+s} rather than a single class) is
  what makes it fire so early: the exposed set X is effectively doubled to
  X u (X - s) before the pigeonhole is applied.

## 3. PROOF

SCRIPT-VERIFIED (finite, exact), and the theorem itself is a two-line argument.

Proof of the theorem. An occurrence of w at slot k0 requires every element of
k0 + X to be an opening of machine M, i.e. for every gear q and every x in X,
k0 + x is not in the blocked pair of q. Writing the pair as {a_q, a_q + s_q} (this
is the standard COV-SAT encoding, K1: gear q blocks k = +-6^{-1} mod q, and shifting
the origin to k0 turns the fixed pair into a free phase a_q = -k0 + 6^{-1} say), the
requirement is a_q != x and a_q + s_q != x for all x in X, i.e.
a_q not in (X mod q) u ((X - s_q) mod q). If that union is all of Z_q there is no
admissible a_q, so no k0 exists. []

The cardinality remark is immediate: the union has at most 2|X| = 2k elements.

## 4. IMPLICATIONS

- INSIDE THE PROJECT. Round 25 read the arity law off one event and registered the
  predictor "A_kill(M->q') >= 5 iff the alternating pair (s, q'-s) is realised at
  machine M". Round 26 pre-registered and tested it at 53->59: the pair (20,39) IS
  realised (witness 5,408,553,654,414,421,963, verified from the definition), and
  the 4-letter and 5-letter alternations are ZERO BY THEOREM. The predictor is
  REFUTED in the direction "pair realised, chain absent"; pair realisability is
  necessary (overlap lemma) and not sufficient. The obstruction replaces it, costs
  nothing, and retrodicts every step including the two the old predictor got right.
- THE FIRST 5-CHAIN WAS NOT THE START OF A TREND. A_kill(47->53) = 5 sat at its
  phase-saturation ceiling exactly. At 53->59 the ceiling drops back to 3, so the
  5-chain SHAPE does not recur; 53 was special, and now there is a reason rather
  than an observation.
- FOR THE MERGE LAW. The merge law consumes depths j <= A_kill + 1. Any bound on
  A_kill that is a closed form in the primes - rather than a SAT campaign per step -
  is exactly the shape a hypothesis-free (D) rung needs, and it is finite, integer
  and per-machine, i.e. kernel-checkable.
- HONEST LIMIT. The ceiling bounds the ALTERNATION only. A_kill is a maximum over
  all legal words, and the padded letters (multiples of q') give words the
  obstruction does not kill - at 37->41, 41->43 and 43->47 the alternation ceiling
  is 2 while A_kill is 3, carried by padded words such as (14,41) and (47,47). So
  this is an upper bound on one family and a screen on the rest, not yet a formula
  for A_kill. The full-level screen (`alt_obstruct_r26.py`) zeroes 3/11, 6/15, 6/15,
  4/19 of the k=3 words and up to 27/41 of a k=4 level by arithmetic alone.

  measured input from that chain.
