Twin Primes by Construction

The one document. Everything is built, not named; each claim says why the construction makes it so. State of the argument on 11 September 2026.

PROVED built here or in the kernel MEASURED zero exceptions on a stated range, no proof THEORISED not proved; if true, closes the conjecture
Part I · The construction

1The line and a gear PROVED

Take the counting numbers 1, 2, 3, … in a row: the line. A gear of size g is the rule "strike every g-th number": it strikes g, 2g, 3g, … and nothing else. A number is struck by a set of gears if at least one strikes it. A prime p is a number above 1 that no smaller number above 1 divides, so it is struck by no gear smaller than p, and by its own gear at p.

2The fold: what gears 2 and 3 leave PROVED

Run the gears 2 and 3. In each strip of six, 6j … 6j+5, gear 2 takes the even ones and gear 3 takes 6j and 6j+3. Only 6j+1 and 6j+5 survive. Pair the survivors as slots (6j−1, 6j+1): two numbers at distance 2; call j the slot's column. Every prime above 3 sits in a slot, and a twin prime pair is a slot with both members prime. A gear g ≥ 5 strikes column j exactly when g divides 6j−1 or 6j+1: two residues per gear, the gear's teeth.

WhyOne strip of six checked by hand covers every strip, because the gears repeat every 6. Kernel: TopMachine.strikesR_iff.

3Only the gears up to the square root decide a prime PROVED

If n < P² and no prime below P strikes n, then n is prime.

WhyIf n = a × b with 1 < a ≤ b, then a² ≤ n < P², so a < P, and the smallest prime factor of a is below P and strikes n. Contradiction. Kernel: OneStepE.blocked_iff_sqrt, CoreLeftover.twin_of_rough.

4Cuts, sections, machines

Set c₁ = 3. Let p_k be the smallest prime at or above c_k, and set c_{k+1} = p_k². Section k is the numbers from c_k up to c_{k+1}: a section runs from a number to about its square. Machine k is the set of gears whose sizes are the primes in section k. Every prime above 2 belongs to exactly one machine, because the sections tile the line from 3 on.

5Inside a section, higher machines only home-strike or echo PROVED

Take n in section k+1, so p_k² ≤ n < p_{k+1}². By 3, n is prime exactly when no prime below p_{k+1} strikes it, and those primes are precisely the gears of machines 1 … k. A gear p ≥ p_{k+1} that strikes n = p × c has c < p_{k+1}: either c = 1 (the gear strikes its own size, a home strike) or c has a prime factor below p_{k+1} that already strikes n (an echo). So a slot of section k+1 left unstruck by machines 1 … k has both members prime: a twin prime pair.

WhyKernel: OneStepE.new_iff, MachineStack.exhaust_home_or_echo, MachineStack.stack_open_iff_twin.

6The hand-up: survivors become gears PROVED

By 4 the gears of machine k+1 are the primes of section k+1; by 5 those are exactly the numbers machines 1 … k leave unstruck. So machine k+1 is built from the survivors of the machines below it, and a twin prime pair in section k+1, two gears of machine k+1 at distance 2, and a slot left open by machines 1 … k are one and the same thing.

7The base PROVED

Machine 1 = {3, 5, 7} has the pairs (3, 5) and (5, 7). Section 2 = [9, 121): the slots machine 1 leaves open are (11,13), (17,19), (29,31), (41,43), (59,61), (71,73), (101,103), (107,109), eight twin pairs, the twin gears of machine 2.

8The step THEORISED

For every k: the gears of machines 1 … k leave at least one slot in section k+1 unstruck. By 6 that is the same as: machine k+1 has two gears at distance 2. In plain words: between every cut and its square there is a twin prime pair.

MEASURED every section along the chain from 3 to 260,467,321 and along every chain from every base to 23. PROVED at three links (Part III.4).

9The conclusion PROVED given 8

Machine 1 has a twin-gear pair (7). By 8 machines 1 … k leave a slot open in section k+1, which by 5 is a twin prime pair and by 6 a twin-gear pair of machine k+1. So every section holds a twin prime pair, the sections tile the line, and there are infinitely many twin primes.

Part II · The one statement, in its exact forms

IIForms of 8, each proved equivalent or sufficient

  • 8a, the section form. Some column j with p_k² ≤ 6j−1 and 6j+1 < p_{k+1}² is struck by no gear g ≤ q, where q is the largest prime below p_{k+1} (just under p_k²). Equivalent to 8 by 2 and 5.
  • 8b, the run form. With a the cut's column (6a+1 = p_k²) and l = (p_{k+1}² − p_k²)/6 the section's length in columns, let L_a be the number of consecutive columns from a on that are each struck by some gear ≤ q. Then 8 ⟺ L_a < l. PROVED Kernel SquareColumn.twin_in_section_iff_L_lt.
  • 8c, the record form (sufficient). Let F(q) be the largest number of consecutive columns anywhere on the line each struck by some gear of {5 … q}. If F(q) < l then 8 holds at that link. Since l ≈ q²/6, the uniform statement F(q) < q²/6 for every prime q gives 8 at every link. PROVED Kernel SquareColumn.section_twin_of_record: the record enters only as a hypothesis.
  • 8d, 8c on the line. Every interval of q² consecutive numbers contains a slot (n, n+2), n ≡ 5 mod 6, with no prime factor ≤ q in either member.
  • 8e, the finer statement (sufficient, not needed). A twin pair between every pair of consecutive prime squares. Implies 8, is far stronger, measured true for every prime to 10⁷. Its own wall is Part IV.3.
Part III · What is proved about 8

III.1Mechanics of the machine that bear on a run of struck columns PROVED

  • The offset-strike law at a square. With p² = 6a+1, a gear g strikes column a+i iff p² ≡ −6i or 2−6i (mod g). Near a square every strike is a statement about p² modulo the gear; a gear can strike offset i for some p only if −6i or 2−6i is a square mod g (the blind classes). Kernel SquareColumn.offset_strike, blind_class.
  • The two-prime lemma. A number below t³ with no prime factor ≤ t is a prime or a product of exactly two primes above t. Kernel CoreLeftover.primeOrSemiprime_of_rough_lt_cube.
  • The core / tail split. On a stretch of L columns the gears ≤ 6L+1 decide the pattern; each larger gear strikes at most one column, and every such strike on a core-open column is a product of two large primes. Kernel TopMachineRecord.loaded_record_rule, CoreLeftover.leftover_eq_card_twins.
  • The depth law. A core-open column below (6L+1)² is a twin. Kernel CoreLeftover.twin_of_rough.
  • The record is an exact cover (law W16), and the parity law for machines whose every gear exceeds 2m+1: F = 2m − (m mod 2), kernel TopMachineCrt.parity_law. The primes never satisfy its hypothesis.
  • The mex form of the next open column in the free regime, kernel TopMachineWalk.mex_form.

III.2What 8 cannot be built from PROVED by construction

  • Counting alone. Shifted gears (progressions a+g, a+2g, …) can strike every slot of a section: 776 of the 1,226 gears below 10⁴ strike the first 60 cycles at q = 5. Any argument using only how many numbers each gear strikes applies to them.
  • Multiples alone. The machine of all primes below p_{k+1} together with the twin primes of section k+1 strikes multiples, has the divisor property, obeys every law on record, and leaves no open slot. Only 4 excludes it: a machine's gears are the primes of its own section.
  • The gear set alone (for 8e). Keep the gears and change only their teeth: at p = 17, 29 and every cut 37 … 53 some two-tooth machine strikes the whole interval between consecutive prime squares; at 7, 11, 13, 19, 23, 31 none does. 8e needs the real teeth.
  • Dilation, hand-up and the square-root rule without the finite fold. The monoid generated by 5 and the primes ≡ 1 mod 6: strikes are multiples, its gears are its own irreducibles, its sections lie between irreducible squares, and 8 fails on it: [25, 961) has 20 columns with both members in it and no twin irreducibles. Each of the three counter-machines breaks exactly one of the three axioms that make the real machine (dilation, the fold 2·3, the hand-up), so each is necessary. The fold enters only through the strike-class law, the sieve's input, which is insufficient at the origin (IV.1).
  • The gear set alone, in full. Thin the primes by exactly the twin lowers. The thinned set has both classes at every scale, is equidistributed in every admissible class, keeps dilation, the square-root rule and the side-swap rule, and its monoid kills every section (361 of 361 finer sections, all three construction sections). Every two-class monoid is transparent: 8 holds on it iff its generators keep a twin lower in the section (proved, 0 mismatches over 7,309 sections). So no property of the gear set is the missing axiom. What the thinning breaks is the line: (101, 103) is open under it and 101 is not a gear. The axiom is the first half of 2, that every survivor of the fold is on the line, a gear or a multiple of a smaller gear.
  • The nesting is true and is not a constraint. The struck set is the disjoint union over gears g of the dilates g·R_g, R_g the open survivors of the machine below g (0 mismatches over 198,798 members in 19 sections): the pattern at the origin is built of scaled copies of the lower patterns. But the union is the same set however it is labelled, so it forbids no cover the real teeth do not already forbid.

III.3The counts, all closed MEASURED

The core's leftover on a stretch of the section's record length is an extreme value of a count (z about −4.5), the same for real phases, random phases and integer gear sets; the minimum leftover is 0 exactly when the core's own record reaches the stretch; supply never binds; the leftover members are primes or products of two tail primes; the twin-free runs sit on the independent-slot prediction at every length; the one band deviation has no counterpart on five other sections.

Part IV · What a proof of 8 must produce

IV.0The certified records against the square

IV.1The length face: F(q) < q²/6

Needed: a bound of exponent 2 on the record of a two-tooth machine with the real teeth, constant 1/6. Known in print: for one tooth per gear the dimension-1 sieve gives exponent 2 (Iwaniec 1978, constant unspecified); for two teeth the dimension-2 sieve stops at exponent 4.27 (sifting limit 4.2665); the free-phase form is Ziller–Morack's Conjecture 6, open, measured true to p_n = 73; lower bounds of Erdős–Rankin and Ford–Green–Konyagin–Maynard–Tao type are of order q log q log log log q. The gap between 2 and 4.27 is the parity barrier in covering form.

Answered, root, with the parity twin built. The strike pattern on a stretch depends only on the phase vector (x mod g), and every vector occurs, so F(q) is the record of a fixed-separation two-tooth family: the real teeth add one thing, the separation 3⁻¹ mod g, worth a factor 1.3 to 1.8 in length over free classes, never an exponent. The parity twin O⁻, the open columns whose members have Liouville product −1, is empty below (q'²−1)/6 > q²/6 while its sieve data is indistinguishable from the open set's to square-root size (0 of 1,326 cells above 3 sd at q = 23 … 53). So at every cut a set with the same sieve inputs violates the target by at least the section's length. The real teeth break the sign symmetry in exactly one place: the origin, below q'², where every open column is a twin. Every section sits there; every record run sits far above it (x/L ≥ 384,679). Any uniform F(q) ≤ Cq² with C < 1/6 already implies twin primes.

IV.2The count face

Every count on record is matched by a machine with no open slot (III.2, III.3). A proof cannot be a count.

IV.3The position face (8e only)

For 8e the interval between consecutive squares is often shorter than the record (at 7 of 11 certified cuts; p = 41: 28 columns against F = 91), so 8e is about where the record's runs sit; the first run of a given length has no floor (at p = 29 it lies below the square); and the gear set alone cannot give it. Root for 8e. Not needed for 8.

IV.3aThe fields

Split the hits by the striker's kind: field 1 the primes, field j the products of exactly j primes ≥ 5, the square field the diagonal of field 2. Exact and proved: every field is the primes dilated, so no field has a location rule the primes lack; no field is periodic though the overlay is; field j hits a right member iff its class −1 factor count is even; dilating a column by g lands its members mirror-symmetric about 6gm, and the symmetric pairs of field j about that axis are exactly the pairs of field j−1 at equal distance, the twin being radius 1; the square field is empty in every finer section and equals the cuts of the construction. The growing mirror symmetry is real (pairs per axis 2, 47, 110,944 along the chain from 3) and is the pairing of the primes about 6m with the twin as its innermost radius. The split is the one a sieve cannot make: the fields are the factor-count strata of the overlay, the sieve sees only the overlay, and the column sign of IV.1 is the parity of the field-index sum. Root.

IV.4The shape of what would close 8

The three faces meet at one place. The count face and the length face are both parity-blocked away from the origin, and the one place where the real teeth are not a free two-tooth choice is the origin: below q'² every open column is a twin, the sign symmetry is broken there and nowhere else, and that is exactly where every section sits.

What would close 8 is a mechanic of the origin: a reason, built from the construction, that the primes below p_{k+1} cannot strike every column between p_k² and p_{k+1}², which uses that these columns lie below p_{k+1}² (the square-root rule), that the gears are the survivors of the sections below (the hand-up), and the finite fold in a way that is not the strike-class law alone (the monoid counter-machine has the other two and still kills a section), and not a count. The one construct named and not yet excluded: a non-count use of the tail pins, the strikes g × m of the section with m a small prime, which the origin forces (below g³ every quotient is prime) and height does not.

Sharper still after the fold lane: the reason cannot be a property of the gear set (both classes, equidistribution, dilation, hand-up in the monoid sense: a thinned prime set has them all and kills every section). It must use that the line is complete, every survivor of the fold being a gear or a multiple of a smaller gear, at phase zero, in a way that is neither a free-phase cover nor a count. The teeth, the classes and the gear set are exhausted.

A closed-form statement of the form "the machine {5 … q} with its real teeth cannot cover q²/6 consecutive columns, because …", where the reason is a proved mechanic (III.1) or a new one built from the construction, and not a count. The one proved mechanic specific to the real teeth is the offset-strike law: every strike is a divisibility of 6j ± 1, so a fully struck run of L columns is an interval of 6L numbers in which every number ≡ ±1 mod 6 has a prime factor ≤ q. Nothing on record turns that into a bound of exponent 2.

VThe walk and the locator (2026-09-13)

The frame adopted from the owner: the window statement alone suffices. If every window (q, q²] holds a twin, twins are infinite and 8 follows (the section from a cut c to P² lies inside the window of machine P ≥ c). No layers, cycles or lookahead. The walls are exact: no gear above q strikes below q²; nothing above q² enters; one square per gear; composites of order at most ⌊2 log₅ q⌋ (exact at 18 machines to 401), orders 4 and up the smooth field, orders 2 and 3 the rest, the order-2 kills mostly a gear times a prime of the window itself.

V.1The mirror walk: steps EXACT

A mirror axis is a multiple of the product M of a gear set containing 2 and 3; the pattern of those gears is symmetric about every multiple of M. A flip about kM sends (n, n+2) to (2kM − n − 2, 2kM − n) and carries every gear dividing 2kM. Composition: a walk about a₁, a₂, … ends at 2A − n − 2 (odd flips) or n + 2A (even), A the alternating sum, certified for exactly the gears dividing A (3000 random walks, 0 violations; stepwise tracking undercounts: 42k₂ − 30k₁ = 66 carries 11). Kill rule: a killed column carries its killer onto the gear pair containing it, matching side (0 violations, 3291 instances at 101). Not yet in the kernel.

V.2The landing family and the lemma EXACT

From home (−1, 1) every walk ends on (2A − 1, 2A + 1), A a multiple of 6, certified for the divisors of A. Lemma (the content of 3): a column in the window that no gear up to q strikes is a twin. A remaining gear h strikes the landing iff 2A ≡ ∓1 (mod h): two classes per gear, read off the roots.

V.3The locator MEASURED to 20000

Rule: A = kM, M = 6 or 30; k the smallest value with the landing in the window avoiding the two teeth of every remaining gear. One flip from home. Every machine from 11 to 20000 lands on a twin (2258 of 2258), just above q. Carrying more gears buys nothing: a mirror carries only gears whose primorial stays below q²/2 (seven by q = 3000 against hundreds); the roots do the work.

The same walk with the square as the landing zone: g the first prime above √q, i the smallest offset no gear below g strikes (h divides neither r² + 6i − 2 nor r² + 6i, r = g mod h: exact, 45,150 checks); every column in (g², gg') missed by the gears below g and by g is a twin (8194 columns to 1500, 0 exceptions); 2258 of 2258 to 20000, offset at most 27 columns. The gears below g are the gears up to √q: the sub-machine locates the machine's twin. Exact laws found on the way: the fixed-offset column after any square is open to 5 iff i ≡ 0, 2 (mod 5), to 7 iff i ≡ 3, 5 (mod 7); other gears strike it only from the square-root classes of −(6i − 2) and −6i; blind gears (neither a residue) are a quarter of the gears at every offset, count (h−1)/4, (h−3)/4, (h−5)/4 by h mod 8.

V.4Termination: the one open statement THEORISED

On the m-line (landing family (12m − 1, 12m + 1), gear h painting m ≡ ∓12⁻¹ (mod h)) the walk lands for q iff some m with 12m in the window is unpainted in every row h ≤ q. Let R(q) be the longest painted run of m below q²/12. The walk terminates whenever R(q) is below the window length (q² − q)/12. Exact to 3001: R = 4, 13, 43, 80, 191, 278 at q = 11, 31, 101, 401, 1009, 3001 against windows 9, 77, 841, 13366, 84756, 750250; open m in the window 3, 14, 100, 906, 4179, 26960. This is 8 in run form on the m-line: the twin machine with teeth ∓12⁻¹, half the columns, the same margin.

V.5Standing

Exact: the frame, the walls, the walk's steps, the landing family, the lemma, the strike law with roots, the blind-gear laws, the order ceiling. Measured and never failing: the locator to 20000 in two forms. Open: one statement, V.4, the record on the m-line below the window for every q, which is 8b in the walk's coordinates.
